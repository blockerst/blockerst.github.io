Personal project · single user · runs on one Mac

wire

A desktop application that reads the newsletters in my own Gmail mailbox, indexes them on my own computer, and answers questions about them — entirely offline.

What it does

I subscribe to more financial and technology newsletters than I can read. wire collects the ones I have explicitly listed by sender address, stores them on my Mac, and lets me ask questions across them: what a given week said about a company, which topics recurred, which tickers were discussed most. The answers cite the emails they came from.

It runs as a scheduled job on a single machine at home. There is no website to sign in to, no account to create, no server that holds anyone's mail. The only person who uses it is me.

How it reaches Gmail

wire signs in with Google OAuth and requests exactly one permission:

ScopeWhy
gmail.readonly To download the newsletters sent by the addresses I have configured. Read-only: wire cannot send, delete, modify, or label anything in the mailbox.

No other scope is requested, and no other Google service is touched. The access can be withdrawn at any time from Google Account → Third-party access.

Where the data goes

Nowhere. Messages are stored on the computer that runs wire and are analysed by a language model running locally on that same computer. Email content is not sent to any external API, is not used to train anything, and is not shared with anyone.
